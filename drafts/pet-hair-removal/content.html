Pet hair wins when the cleanup starts with the couch. By the time hair is woven into the cushions, it takes a lint roller, a vacuum and a good deal of patience to get it back out. The faster route works in the other direction: get loose hair off the pet first, then pick up what's left every day with the right tool for each surface, before it has a chance to build up. Done that way, keeping up takes about 15 minutes a day, plus one longer reset a week.

By Avery Linden · Updated November 2026 · Kids & Pets

The short version

Brush your pet a few times a week, and daily during heavy shedding, so loose hair goes into a brush instead of onto the furniture. Then spend about 15 minutes a day on a quick sweep: a rubber glove or squeegee over the couch, a vacuum or dry mop on the floors where your pet sleeps, and a shake-out of their bed or throw. Wash pet bedding and throws weekly, run them through the dryer before the washer to pull loose hair off, and clean the lint trap and vacuum filter more often than you think you need to.

Step 1: Start with the pet, not the furniture

Every hair you brush out is one you don't have to pick off a cushion later. Texas A&M's veterinary school suggests brushing dogs at least three to four times a week for 5 to 10 minutes, and the American Kennel Club notes that heavy shedders may need brushing several times a week or even daily during peak shedding. Do it outside, on a porch or in a tiled room you can sweep, so the hair doesn't simply move from the dog to the rug.

Match the brush to the coat. Short, smooth coats do well with a rubber curry brush or grooming glove; double coats usually need an undercoat rake or deshedding tool; long coats need a slicker brush and a comb for tangles. Cats can be brushed the same way, a few minutes at a time, ending before they lose patience.

Shedding usually rises with changes in daylight and temperature, so expect more hair in spring and fall. If your pet is suddenly shedding much more than usual, losing hair in patches, or scratching a lot, that's a reason to call the vet rather than buy a new vacuum.

Step 2: Run the 15-minute daily sweep

The daily sweep isn't a full clean. It's a quick pass over the places your pet actually spends time, done at the same time each day so hair never piles up.

Step 3: Use the right tool for each surface

No single gadget handles everything. Rubber works on fabric because it grips hair and rolls it into clumps; a vacuum works on floors; sticky rollers are best for quick touch-ups on clothes.

  • Upholstery: a damp rubber glove, a rubber pet-hair brush or a window squeegee, pulled in one direction. Finish with the vacuum's upholstery tool.
  • Carpet and rugs: a rubber carpet rake loosens hair stuck in the pile before you vacuum. Go slowly, in overlapping passes, in more than one direction.
  • Hard floors: a dry microfiber mop or vacuum. Sweeping with a broom tends to send fine hair into the air and corners.
  • Car seats: the same rubber brush, then a handheld vacuum. A washable seat cover saves time if your dog rides often.
  • Clothes: a lint roller or reusable lint brush, kept by the door and in the car.

If anyone in your home has pet allergies, choose a vacuum with a sealed HEPA filter, since some vacuums blow fine particles back out the exhaust, and talk to a doctor or allergist about what else helps. Pet allergies are usually triggered by dander, saliva and urine proteins, not the hair itself, so a pet-free bedroom often matters more than any tool.

Step 4: Get hair out of laundry before you wash

Water makes pet hair cling to fabric, so a hairy blanket that goes straight into the washer often comes out still hairy, and leaves hair in the drum for the next load. Run pet bedding, throws and very hairy clothes through the dryer for about 10 minutes on low or no heat first. The tumbling loosens hair and the lint trap catches it.

Worked example: the weekly dog blanket

Shake the blanket outside. Dryer, 10 minutes on air fluff with two or three wool dryer balls. Empty the lint trap, which will be full. Wash as the care label says, with a mesh pet-hair catcher in the drum if you have one. Dry, then clean the lint trap again and wipe the washer drum and door seal with a damp cloth so the next load stays clean.

Check your dryer's lint screen after every load of pet laundry. A clogged screen slows drying, and lint buildup in dryers is a known fire risk.

Step 5: Make the weekly reset count

Once a week, do the jobs the daily sweep skips. Pull the couch cushions and vacuum underneath them. Vacuum the stairs and the edges of rugs. Wash all pet bedding and the throws on furniture your pet uses. Clean or replace the vacuum filter and cut hair off the brush roll with scissors, since a tangled roller picks up far less. Wipe baseboards and vents where hair collects.

This is also a good time to brush your pet for longer, trim around the paws if your groomer recommends it, and check the coat and skin for anything unusual.

Step 6: Set up the house so hair has fewer places to land

A few small changes cut down the daily work. Put a washable throw on your pet's favorite seat and wash the throw instead of the sofa. Give your pet a bed of their own in the room where the family spends evenings, since pets tend to follow people. Keep a basket by the couch with the rubber brush and lint roller so the daily sweep takes seconds to start. Tightly woven fabrics, leather and microfiber tend to release hair more easily than textured weaves and velvet, which is worth remembering the next time you buy a sofa or a throw.

What picks up pet hair best from a couch?

Rubber is the most reliable option for most upholstery: a rubber glove, a rubber pet-hair brush or a squeegee, pulled in one direction so the hair rolls into clumps. Follow with the vacuum's upholstery tool. Check the care tag first on delicate fabrics.

How often should I brush my dog to cut down on shedding?

Texas A&M's veterinary school suggests at least three to four times a week for 5 to 10 minutes. The AKC notes heavy shedders may need daily brushing during peak shedding season. Your vet or groomer can tell you what suits your dog's coat.

Why is my washing machine full of pet hair?

Wet hair clings to fabric and the drum. Shaking items out and running them through the dryer for about 10 minutes before washing removes much of the loose hair, and wiping the drum and door seal afterward keeps it from moving to the next load.

Is sudden heavy shedding normal?

Seasonal increases are normal, but a sudden change, bald patches, sores or lots of scratching can point to a health problem. Call your veterinarian rather than trying to manage it with cleaning alone.
